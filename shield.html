Common Denial Claim

"It was a civil war, not genocide"

Legal Determination

The International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR) established in multiple verdicts that the events constituted genocide with specific intent to destroy Tutsi as a group.

ICTR Verdicts UN Documentation

Evidence of Intent

Radio broadcasts, government documents, and perpetrator testimonies show explicit calls for extermination of Tutsi specifically, not just political opponents.

Systematic Nature

The killings followed specific patterns: lists of Tutsi were prepared, roadblocks checked identity cards, and specific groups were targeted regardless of political affiliation.

Demographic Studies

Multiple independent demographic studies estimate approximately 1,000,000 Tutsi were killed between April and July 1994, representing about 75% of the Tutsi population in Rwanda.

Census Data

Comparison of pre-1994 and post-1994 census data shows dramatic demographic changes specifically affecting the Tutsi population.

False Equivalence

"There was violence on both sides"

Legal Distinction

ICTR and Gacaca courts made clear distinctions between genocide crimes (targeting Tutsi as a group) and other crimes committed during the period.

ICTR Classification Gacaca Categories

Different Intent

Genocide requires specific intent to destroy a group. Other violence, while tragic, did not meet this legal threshold or demonstrate the same systematic planning.

Legal Analysis Intent Documentation

Scale and System

The genocide was state-planned and systematically executed against Tutsi civilians, while other violence was different in scale, organization, and targeting.
